# Patient Initial Engagement — Interaction Design (v0.1, for review)

**Status:** DRAFT for John's review. Four product decisions below are marked
**[RECOMMENDED — John's call]**; everything else follows from them. The
"Developer notes" section at the end maps each decision to the code it touches.

**Scope:** the patient persona's first session — from the moment the chat opens
through the first substantive answer. Provider-persona interaction design is a
separate, later document.

---

## 1. Design principles

A patient opening this chat is often worried, sometimes scared, and has never
seen this product before. The first 30 seconds have exactly three jobs:

1. **Safety** — an emergency must exit to 911/988 immediately (already built).
2. **Orientation** — say plainly what this is, what it is not (no diagnosis,
   no treatment), and that it is guided by Catholic teaching.
3. **One easy next step** — never leave the patient staring at an empty box.

Standing rules that follow:

- **The AI speaks first.** Today the pipeline is purely reactive; the patient
  must guess what to type. That changes.
- **Data minimization by design.** We never ask who they are. The greeting and
  prompts are written so patients aren't invited to overshare; identity is
  never needed for the patient persona.
- **Doctrine is load-bearing, not decorative.** Catholic identity is stated
  once, up front, honestly — then the doctrinal gate does its work invisibly
  until a topic actually implicates it.
- **Deterministic where it counts.** The greeting is served from configuration,
  not generated by an LLM: zero cost, zero latency, zero chance of an
  off-brand first impression, nothing for the arbiter to review.

---

## 2. Decision 1 — The opening move

**[RECOMMENDED — John's call]: Warm greeting + guided paths.** The AI opens
with a short welcome and 3–4 tappable starting paths. Alternatives considered:
open-ended greeting only (more human, leans harder on the router), or keeping
it reactive (status quo, first impression left to chance).

### Draft greeting copy (patient persona, MyCatholicDoctor-brandable)

> Welcome — I'm the {partner_name} health assistant, here to give you clear,
> trustworthy health information guided by Catholic teaching.
>
> I can help you:
>
> - 🩺 **Find a Catholic doctor** who shares your values
> - 📖 **Understand a condition** or prepare questions for an appointment
> - 🔬 **Explore clinical trials** and emerging treatment options
> - ✝️ **See what the Church teaches** on a health topic
>
> I'm not a doctor and I can't diagnose or treat anything — think of me as
> preparation for real care, not a replacement for it. **If this is an
> emergency, call 911 now.**
>
> What's on your mind?

Why this shape:

- **Each bullet maps 1:1 to an existing agent** (referral / engagement /
  concierge / doctrinal). A patient who taps a chip gives the router a
  near-perfect classification signal; a patient who ignores the chips and
  types freely loses nothing.
- The greeting carries the **full disclaimer** and the **emergency exit**, so
  both are on record before the patient's first word (see Decision 3).
- `{partner_name}` is a config value — this becomes per-tenant branding in
  Phase 2 with no redesign.

## 3. Decision 2 — Faith tone at first contact

**[RECOMMENDED — John's call]: Warm-professional, faith named once.** The
greeting states plainly that the service is guided by Catholic teaching — it
is the differentiator, and the partners are Catholic-branded — but the working
voice is warm, plain-language professionalism. Faith language surfaces
naturally when doctrine is actually implicated (where the arbiter engages),
not as ambient decoration. Alternatives: overtly pastoral throughout (strong
identity, heavy for someone who just needs a doctor's name), or neutral until
asked (weakest differentiation).

### Voice specification (applies to every patient-facing turn)

- Plain language at roughly an 8th-grade reading level; define any medical
  term the moment it's used.
- Warm, unhurried, never alarmist. Acknowledge worry before information
  ("That's a reasonable thing to be concerned about — here's what's known…").
- Next steps are always framed as **questions for their licensed clinician**,
  never as instructions (this mirrors the engagement agent's existing rules).
- Never performatively pious, never clinical-cold. When a topic touches
  Catholic teaching, say so directly and cite it — that's the moment the
  identity earns its keep.

## 4. Decision 3 — Disclaimer cadence

**[RECOMMENDED — John's call]: First message + contextual.** Today the same
disclaimer sentence trails **every** reply, which trains patients to skim past
it by turn three. Proposed:

| Where | What appears |
|---|---|
| Greeting (turn 0) | Full disclaimer, verbatim, plus the 911 line |
| Content-triggered | Short reminder appended only when the reply discusses symptoms, medications, or treatment options |
| Chat UI (persistent) | Small fixed footer: "Educational information — not medical advice." (widget/Phase 3 requirement, already consistent with the master doc's "non-removable disclaimers") |
| Emergency / doctrinal replies | Keep their existing dedicated notices, unchanged |

Alternatives: every message (status quo — simplest to defend to counsel,
weakest attention economics), or a one-time "I understand" acknowledgment
gate (strongest consent record, adds friction before the first word; can be
revisited for the provider persona, where a consent record matters more).

**Counsel checkpoint:** cadence choice should be confirmed during the Phase 5
HIPAA-counsel review; the code change keeps "every message" available as a
config fallback so reverting is a switch, not a rewrite.

## 5. Decision 4 — Handling the vague first message

**[RECOMMENDED — John's call]: One clarifying question, then answer.** When
the first message is vague ("I'm worried about my health"), the AI asks
exactly one focused, warm question, then commits to a full answer on the
patient's reply — it never stacks questions or slides into a form-like
intake. Alternatives: answer-first (no friction, generic output), or a
structured mini-intake (best information, feels like a form, every question
is a drop-off point).

### Example first exchange (target experience)

> **Patient:** i've been having stomach problems and honestly i'm scared
>
> **Assistant:** I'm sorry — being scared about your health is a heavy thing
> to carry, and looking for good information is a sensible first step. So I
> can point you in the most useful direction: is this something new in the
> last few weeks, or an ongoing problem you're trying to get answers about?
>
> **Patient:** ongoing, like 6 months, my doctor hasn't figured it out
>
> **Assistant:** *(full engagement-agent answer: plain-language overview of
> how unexplained GI symptoms are typically worked up, what's established vs.
> uncertain, then 3–5 specific questions to bring to the next appointment —
> e.g. "Would a referral to a gastroenterologist make sense at this point?" —
> and what records to bring. Short contextual disclaimer. If they later ask
> for a doctor, the referral agent takes over with Catholic-aligned matches.)*

Note what the assistant did **not** do: ask their name, age, or history
beyond what was volunteered; give a diagnosis; stack three questions at once.

## 6. Emergency experience (no decision needed — small additions)

The existing short-circuit (keyword check before everything, even auth) is
the right structure and stays. Two additions:

- The greeting itself carries the 911 line, so the exit is visible before the
  first message (covered in Decision 1).
- The emergency reply should render with distinct visual treatment in the
  widget (Phase 3), not as an ordinary chat bubble.

Known limitation to track (not this iteration): the keyword list is
English-only and deliberately conservative; expansion stays a
clinician-reviewed task, as the code comments already require.

---

## 7. Engineering gap this design exposes (fix regardless of decisions)

**The router classifies each message in isolation.** `ChatRequest.history`
exists but is unused in routing, so a follow-up like "tell me more about
that" can be misrouted mid-conversation. The initial-engagement design makes
multi-turn conversations the norm, so routing must see recent history
(de-identified where the mode requires it). This is a correctness fix, not a
style choice.

## 8. Developer notes — what this changes in code

| Change | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Serve the greeting | New `GET /welcome` (or a `welcome` payload in the demo/widget bootstrap) | Deterministic, from config: greeting text + structured `suggested_paths[]` (label, hint, maps-to-intent). Per-persona now; per-tenant in Phase 2. No LLM call, no arbitration needed. |
| Suggested-path chips | `ChatResponse`-adjacent schema | Chips send a normal `message` plus a routing hint the router may trust at high confidence. Free text unaffected. |
| Disclaimer cadence | `app/compliance/safety.py` + `Sovereign._finalize` | `disclaimer_for(persona)` becomes cadence-aware (turn 0 vs. content-triggered); add `DISCLAIMER_MODE=every_message\|contextual` env switch with `every_message` as the fallback so counsel can revert it with config. |
| Voice upgrade | Small-talk system prompt in `orchestrator.py`; `_SYSTEM` in `engagement_agent.py` | Encode Section 3's voice spec; add the one-clarifying-question rule (Section 5) to the engagement agent. |
| History-aware routing | `app/sovereign/router.py` | Include the last N turns in the classification context (de-identified in LITERAL mode, matching the existing invariant). |
| Tests | `tests/test_pipeline.py` | Greeting served without auth; chip → expected intent; disclaimer cadence per mode; vague-message → clarifying question (mockable); history-aware routing regression. |

None of this moves the PHI boundary, the emergency short-circuit, the
persona gate, or the arbitration flow — it is a conversation layer on top of
the existing safety and doctrine structure.
